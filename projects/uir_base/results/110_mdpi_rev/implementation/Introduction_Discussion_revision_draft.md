## Introduction

Language-model outputs and executable authority should be evaluated separately.
A syntactically valid answer may still bind a wrong entity, period, unit, value,
or source. Existing controlled languages, policy engines, structured generation,
and agent-runtime enforcement provide important parts of this separation. Our
study investigates a narrower combination: a typed policy/evidence contract
shared by supported Korean and English restricted frontends, with explicit
accepted-answer and refusal states.

The study design is AI-only following an explicit amendment. The financial
benchmark is constructed, trusted-slot metadata is available to historical
execution, and previous annotation selection was post-hoc. Supplementary AI
reviews are not independent human labels. Raw-only conformance applies to a
declared restricted grammar; this study does not establish free natural-language
semantic accuracy or independently validate all source-fact truth.

## Discussion

The deterministic trusted-slot template is a substantive control, not a weak
baseline. On the historical eligible set it completes 48 more cases than the
SLM-mediated system while matching its supported-answer coverage. Thus the
observations favor treating a generative renderer as optional where trusted
facts suffice. Evidence projection and policy boundaries may explain grounded
outputs without requiring a unique typed-IR accuracy advantage.

Historical HaluEval and D1 corrections distinguish literal task labels, strict
format validity, exact-field support, and acceptance. Missing labels must not
be replaced with a favorable default. Structured-claim zero-event rates do not
certify unassessed native prose. Corpus-family bootstrap intervals account for
recorded dependence, but unrecorded template lineage and document overlap remain.
Batch-shared historical timings cannot establish request-level tail latency or
transformer acceleration.

New local GPU experiments use separately recorded GGUF artifacts and decoding
protocols. They must not be relabeled as replications of historical FP16/NF4
results. The Qwen3-1.7B prospective comparison replaces the unexecuted Qwen2.5-7B
arm only by an explicit new protocol; different sizes, tokenizers and 4-bit
formats prevent a parameter- or quantization-matched interpretation.
